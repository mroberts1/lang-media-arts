## General course description

This course offers an intensive, introductory survey of the foundations of media criticism and theory pertaining to film, television, recorded music, and digital culture. We will explore such germinal areas as the Mass Culture Debates, Cultural Studies, Black Media Studies, Political Economy, Structuralism and Semiotics, Marxist and Feminist media theory, Postcolonial theory, Asian American and Queer media theory, with related forays into theories of Authorship, Genre, and Star Studies. Our investigations will be driven by the following concerns and questions:

1. Aesthetics: What are the formal components of media art and entertainment? How can we appreciate media art across a range of mediums and contexts?
2. Ideology and hegemony: What is mass culture's role in defining "reality," including the social power relationships underpinning various social identities related to ethnicity/race, gender, sexuality, class, disability, national/regional identity, among others?
3. Cultural representations: How is "reality" depicted or constructed in media art, and in turn, what are the power relationships reflected by such representations?
4. Cultural/Social/Historical practices: How and why do audiences consume and interpret mass media art/entertainment, in particular cultural, social, and historical contexts?
5. Self-critique: How do media criticism and theory represent and address different constituencies? What are the limits and problems of media criticism and theory, historically and currently, with respect to inclusive representation and practical application?

## Course and learning objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Have a rudimentary understanding of foundational concepts as well as critical and theoretical approaches to analyzing media art.
2. Have a rudimentary understanding of ethically-based, de-centered scholarly and creative work reflective of under-represented perspectives.
3. Apply theoretical tools to critique and analyze media art as well as media criticism and theory.
4. Recognize the applicability of theory and criticism to creative practice, and, the impact of creative practices on the theory and criticism of media artforms.
5. Progress as a critically-aware media artist empowered to confront the complexities of media art.
6. Progress toward professional experience: facing tasks that are not necessarily easy or immediately interesting; doing good work and handing it on time.
7. (Bonus) Locate the fun of analyzing media art, its meanings and functions.

### 2. Reading Discussion Notes: Muddiest Point/Clearest Point

After thoughtfully conducting the reading due for a particular class meeting:

1. Choose one quotation that reflects the least clear idea of the reading for you: something you want to clarify through further discussion.
2. Choose one quotation that reflects the clearest idea of the reading, for you: something you want to discuss and apply further.
3. Type or write out the quotes along with the author and title of the reading, identifying which is the "muddiest" and which is the "clearest."
4. Bring this to every class to spur discussion.

Ways to avoid academic misconduct:

1. Do your own work.
2. Cite sources in every stage: taking notes, brainstorming, outlining, and writing drafts.

4. In your writing, elucidate your thought process, how you are building on critics' ideas.
