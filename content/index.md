## Course description

What is the essence of the moving image, and why have humans found it so powerfully compelling for the past 125 years? What is the relationship between such images and reality, identity, art, and other media? What drives us to watch screen media, and how do we make sense of what we view? What is screen media's function in society? The purpose of this course is to explore some of the theoretical concepts and contexts fundamental to the study of screen media as an expressive medium and as a subject of critical analysis and scholarly inquiry. We will explore ways of seeing screen media from a variety of perspectives, taking into consideration the specificity of forms and styles; screen media as an industry and as an art form; the relationship between entertainment and ideology; the globalization and digitalization of media; and the contribution of screen media to understandings of gender, race, sexuality, nationality, and humanity itself.

## Learning outcomes

- Students will become familiar with key concepts in media/cultural studies and significant theoretical frameworks, and will gain an understanding of how to critically engage scholarly work to understand the main contributions and limitations therein.
- Students will learn to rigorously apply these concepts and frameworks to their own analyses of media, in so doing demonstrating their ability to apply theoretical information, to perform close readings of cultural texts, to conduct and rigorously apply reputable research, and to communicate ideas effectively in written, oral, and audiovisual presentation.
- Students will develop recognition of ways that media is imbricated with forms of difference, inequities, and power structures, and of media's potential to effect change.

## Assignments & Evaluation

Prompts for all assignments will be distributed well in advance of due dates. Apart from weekly posts (to be submitted as text entries on Canvas), written assignments should be formatted in (rather than converted to) Microsoft Word and submitted by uploading in .doc/.docx format in the Assignments section of our Canvas site.

**Sequence Analysis** (2 x 15%)

- Opening, closing, or mid-sequence screening and analysis. Solo or joint
- A short written analysis of the film in question (3-4 pages double-spaced, 750-1000 words) is due by the Monday following the analysis
- Presenters should meet with me during office hour on Tuesday afternoon to discuss choice of film/TV sequence

**Alternative Soundtrack Project** (15%)

- Details will be provided

**Analytical Essay, Video Essay, or Web Essay** (25%)

- Analytical Essay: 8-10 pages, with bibliography
- Video Essay: ~10 minutes
- Web Essay: published online, equivalent of 6-8 pages, with embedded images, clips (help will be provided)

**Discussion posts** (15%)

- Minimum 10 posts in Discussion forums on Canvas required over the semester
- No fixed length but up to 250 words commenting either on reading or screening assignments for the week
- Due weekly before Tuesday class
- You may post late up to the end of the week in question. However, please note that you cannot post make-up comments after that week is over

**Engagement** (15%): includes punctuality, active participation in class discussion, individual conferences
